# Code of Conduct Updates

Our engineering Code of Conduct was last revised more than three years ago. Since then, our team has more than doubled, we've moved to a hybrid model across several time zones, and much of our work now happens in pull requests, incident channels, and async threads instead of conference rooms. The old document didn't reflect how we work today, so we've rewritten it.

This post covers what changed, why, and what it means for your work.

## Why We Revisited It

A code of conduct only works if people read it, understand it, and trust it. Earlier this year we ran an anonymous survey across the engineering organization. Three themes came up again and again:

1. **The scope felt unclear.** Many people didn't know whether the policy covered code review comments, Slack DMs, or behavior at external conferences.
2. **The reporting process felt opaque.** Several respondents said they had considered reporting something but didn't know what would happen next, or worried it would go nowhere.
3. **Technical spaces had their own friction.** Dismissive review comments, blame during incidents, and gatekeeping in design discussions came up far more often than anything happening face to face.

We formed a working group of volunteers from different teams, levels, and locations to draft the update. The draft went through two rounds of open comment before being finalized.

## What's Changed

### Expanded and Explicit Scope

The Code of Conduct now explicitly applies to:

- Code reviews, pull request discussions, and commit messages
- Issue trackers, design docs, and RFC comments
- Incident response channels and postmortems
- Internal chat, including direct messages
- Company-sponsored events, conferences where you represent the company, and our open source projects

If you're interacting with colleagues or the community in a work capacity, it applies.

### Code Review Guidelines

We've added a section on review etiquette. The short version: **critique the code, not the person.** Concretely, that means:

- Explain the *why* behind requested changes instead of issuing bare directives.
- Label non-blocking suggestions clearly (we recommend a `nit:` or `optional:` prefix).
- Don't make dismissive remarks about someone's experience level or background.
- Ask questions when something is unclear instead of assuming a mistake.

Rigorous review still matters. Reviewers should keep holding a high bar. The point is that rigor and respect don't conflict.

### Blameless Incident Culture

Our postmortems were already meant to be blameless, but the expectation wasn't written into the Code of Conduct. Now it is. During and after incidents, we focus on systems, processes, and contributing factors, not on individuals. Publicly assigning fault during an active incident, or naming and shaming in a postmortem, is a conduct issue.

### A Clearer Reporting Process

This was the most requested change. You now have several ways to report a concern:

- Directly to your manager or any engineering manager you trust
- To the People team through the dedicated reporting form
- Through an **anonymous reporting option** run by a third-party provider

We've also committed to timelines. Every report will be acknowledged within **two business days**, and the reporter will get a status update within **ten business days**. Where privacy allows, we'll tell reporters how their concern was resolved, not just that it was "handled."

### Explicit Anti-Retaliation Protections

Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, or who takes part in an investigation, is now a standalone violation with its own consequences.

### Transparency on Enforcement

Each quarter we'll publish an aggregated, anonymized summary of reports received and how they were resolved. We want everyone to see that the process works, without compromising anyone's privacy.

## What Hasn't Changed

The core principles stay the same: treat people with respect, assume good intent, welcome different perspectives, and take responsibility for the impact of your actions. The update makes those principles more concrete and easier to act on.

## What We're Asking of You

- **Read the updated document.** It's linked from the engineering handbook and takes about ten minutes.
- **Complete the short acknowledgment** in the internal portal by the end of the month.
- **Bring it into your team rituals.** Managers will get a brief discussion guide for an upcoming team meeting.
- **Keep the feedback coming.** This is a living document. The working group will review it every six months, and you can propose changes anytime through the feedback form.

## Thank You

Thanks to everyone who filled out the survey, joined a listening session, or left comments on the draft. Those comments shaped nearly every section of the new document.

Our best work happens when people feel safe to ask questions, challenge ideas, and admit mistakes. This update is meant to make that kind of environment the norm.

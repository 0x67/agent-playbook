# Participate in the 2016 Go User Survey and Company Questionnaire

Every language ecosystem has a story told by its numbers: how many people use it, what they build with it, what frustrates them, and what would make their day-to-day work better. For Go, a large share of that story comes from you. This year, we're asking you to help write it by taking part in the 2016 Go User Survey and the companion Company Questionnaire.

## Why this survey matters

Go has grown quickly since its 1.0 release. It now powers infrastructure at companies of every size, from small startups running a handful of services to large organizations operating thousands of them. With that growth comes a wider range of needs. A developer writing a command-line tool has different priorities than a team running latency-sensitive services in production, and both differ from someone who is just learning the language.

The Go team can't guess at all of these needs from the outside. Mailing list threads and issue tracker discussions are valuable, but they reflect the people who are already vocal. A survey reaches the quieter majority: the engineer who uses Go every day and never files a bug, the team that quietly adopted it last quarter, and the person who tried it, hit a wall, and moved on.

Your responses help answer questions like:

- Which parts of the toolchain are working well, and which slow you down?
- How are teams managing dependencies and vendoring today?
- What platforms and environments do you deploy to?
- Which libraries and areas of the standard library need the most attention?
- What keeps people from adopting Go, or from using it more widely?

The answers feed directly into prioritization. When a pain point shows up consistently across hundreds of responses, it becomes much harder to ignore, and much easier to justify investing in.

## Two forms, two perspectives

This year there are two separate pieces to fill out.

**The Go User Survey** is for individual developers. It asks about your experience with the language, your tools, your workflow, and your wishes for the future. It takes only a few minutes, and you don't need to be an expert. Newcomers are especially valuable, since they see rough edges that veterans have long since learned to work around.

**The Company Questionnaire** is for organizations. It's meant to capture how Go is used in a business context: what kinds of systems it runs, how it fits into existing stacks, and what a company needs in order to feel confident adopting it further. If you have insight into your team's or company's use of Go, please consider filling this out in addition to your personal survey. One response per company is plenty, so it's worth coordinating with colleagues.

## What happens with your answers

Survey data is only useful if it's handled responsibly. Individual responses are used to understand aggregate trends, not to single out people or companies. Results are summarized and shared back with the community so everyone can see what was learned, and so the discussion about Go's direction is grounded in evidence rather than anecdote.

Seeing the results published is one of the more rewarding parts of the process. It's a chance to compare your own experience against the broader picture and to notice where your concerns are widely shared.

## How you can help

Participating takes only a few steps:

1. **Take the user survey.** Set aside a few minutes and answer honestly. Candid feedback, including criticism, is the most useful kind.
2. **Share the company questionnaire** with whoever is best placed to answer it at your organization, or fill it out yourself if that's you.
3. **Spread the word.** Post the links to your team chat, your local meetup group, or your social feeds. The more varied the responses, the more representative the results. Reaching people outside the usual circles, such as those new to Go or working in less visible industries, makes the data better for everyone.

## A small investment with a big return

It's easy to assume that one response won't change anything. In practice, surveys like this are built from exactly those individual contributions. Ten minutes of your time can influence the tooling, documentation, and priorities that shape the language for the coming year.

Whether you've been writing Go since the early days or you shipped your first program last week, your perspective counts. Please take the survey, share it with your colleagues, and help make Go better for everyone who uses it.

Thank you for taking the time to participate.

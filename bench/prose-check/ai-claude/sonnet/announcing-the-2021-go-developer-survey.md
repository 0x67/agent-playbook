# Announcing the 2021 Go Developer Survey

*Help us shape the future of Go by sharing how you use it.*

Every year, we ask the people who write Go to tell us how it's going. What's working? What's frustrating? What do you wish the language, the tooling, and the ecosystem did differently? Today we're opening the 2021 Go Developer Survey, and we'd love your voice in it.

The survey takes roughly 10–15 minutes to complete, it's anonymous, and it's open to everyone, whether you've been writing Go since before 1.0 or you wrote your first `func main()` last week.

## Why we run this survey

Engineering decisions made on intuition alone tend to reflect the loudest voices in the room. Issue trackers, mailing lists, and conference hallways all provide valuable signal, but they skew toward people who are already deeply engaged with the project. The annual survey gives us something different: a broader, more representative picture of how Go is actually used in the field.

Past survey results have directly influenced our priorities. Feedback about dependency management helped drive investment in modules. Persistent requests around error handling and generic programming kept those topics on the roadmap and informed the design discussions that followed. When you tell us that build times, documentation, or editor support are getting in your way, those answers show up in our planning.

## What's new this year

We've made several changes to make the survey more useful and easier to complete.

**A tighter focus on workflows.** Rather than asking only about what you use, we're asking more about how you work: how you find and evaluate packages, how you debug production issues, and how you test and deploy services. These questions help us understand where the real friction is, not just where the features are.

**Better coverage of newer features.** With generics moving from proposal toward implementation, we want to understand what you expect from them, where you think they'll be most useful, and what concerns you have. We're also asking about your experience with modules, fuzzing, and workspace tooling.

**Questions about the community.** A healthy ecosystem depends on more than code. We're asking about how welcoming you find Go spaces, what barriers newcomers face, and what would help you contribute more.

**Shorter and clearer.** We cut questions that produced little actionable data over the past few years and rewrote ambiguous ones. If a question confused people in the last round, we tried to fix it.

## What we'll do with your answers

Once the survey closes, we'll analyze the responses and publish a summary of the results on this blog. We aim to share the findings in aggregate, including where responses were mixed or surprising, and to be candid about what we plan to act on and what we can't address right now.

We'll also make an effort to account for who responded. Survey data reflects the people who take the survey, so we'll note where our respondents may differ from the broader Go community and treat conclusions accordingly.

Your individual responses are never published. We report on aggregated trends only, and free-text comments are used internally to help us understand themes rather than being quoted with identifying details.

## How you can help beyond taking it

The value of this survey depends on who answers it. If the only respondents are people who already read our blog, we'll learn a lot about people like us and very little about everyone else. A few ways to help:

- **Share it with your team.** Especially colleagues who use Go but don't follow the community closely.
- **Pass it along to newcomers.** People who recently started with Go often notice problems that experienced users have stopped seeing.
- **Reach beyond your usual circles.** Developers who use Go alongside other languages, work in specialized domains, or come from underrepresented groups give us perspective we can't get any other way.
- **Be specific in free-text answers.** "Error handling is annoying" is useful. "Wrapping errors across package boundaries loses context in our logging pipeline, and here's what we do about it" is far more useful.

## Timeline

The survey is open for about three weeks. Responses are collected through the closing date announced on the survey page, after which we'll begin analysis. Expect a results post in the weeks that follow.
